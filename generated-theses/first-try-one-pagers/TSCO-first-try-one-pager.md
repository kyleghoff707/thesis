### Company Summary

Tractor Supply Company is the largest rural lifestyle retailer in the United States. The company operates approximately 2,270 stores in 49 states, serving recreational farmers, ranchers, and rural homeowners. Product categories include livestock and pet food, hardware and tools, seasonal goods (mowers, heaters, fencing), work clothing, and truck/trailer/towing accessories. Tractor Supply also operates Petsense by Tractor Supply, a chain of pet specialty stores.

**How they make money:** Retail sales of consumable, usable, and edible (C.U.E.) products — primarily livestock feed, pet food, and other recurring-need items that drive repeat traffic. Hardware, tools, and seasonal goods are higher-margin but less frequent purchases. Revenue is almost entirely US-based with no meaningful international exposure.

**Why they might be durable:** Dominant position in an underserved niche (rural retail) with no direct national competitor. Walmart and Home Depot don't replicate the full rural lifestyle assortment. High percentage of consumable products (feed, pet food) drives recurring revenue. Store economics are strong — smaller footprints than big-box retailers with lower build-out costs. Customer base is loyal and habitual.

**Why they might not be:** Growth is decelerating as the store footprint matures — the easy new-store growth is behind them. Same-store sales have been under pressure. The business is entirely US-domestic with no international optionality. Amazon and online competitors could erode non-consumable categories. A sustained rural economic downturn (ag commodity prices, drought) could impact the customer base.

**Clarity test:** Can you explain what this company does? Yes — they sell feed, tools, and farm supplies to rural Americans through retail stores. Simple and understandable. **PASS.**

#### Growth Assessment

**Positives:**
- Revenue has grown every single year for 7 consecutive years — no declines
- Equity/book value growth is accelerating: 6.1% (5yr) → 8.1% (3yr) → 13.7% (1yr)
- Operating cash flow is accelerating: 3.2% (5yr) → 6.4% (3yr) → 15.1% (1yr)
- Gross margins are slowly expanding (34.4% → 36.4% over 7 years) — improving pricing power
- ROE has been above 35% for 7 straight years, averaging ~46% — world-class
- Long-term debt has been a flat $150M for 7 years — zero incremental borrowing to fund growth
- FCF improved from $594M → $740M with a steady upward trend since 2021

**Concerns:**
- Revenue growth is clearly decelerating: 7.9% (5yr) → 3.0% (3yr) → 4.3% (1yr) — none of these are double-digit
- Net income is essentially flat over 3 years (0.2% CAGR) and declined -0.5% in the most recent year
- Operating margins are compressing: peaked at 10.3% (2021), now 9.5% (2025)
- Net margins trending down: 7.8% (2021) → 7.1% (2025)
- ROE and ROIC, while still exceptional, are trending down from 2022 peaks (53.3% → 42.5%)
- CapEx as a % of operating CF is elevated (~55%) due to heavy store buildout — consuming cash flow

**Verdict:** This is a mature, high-quality business with elite capital returns but decelerating top-line and flat bottom-line growth. Revenue has not hit double-digit growth in the 3-year or 1-year windows. Earnings are essentially plateauing. The company is still growing, but the growth rate is slowing as the business matures. This is not a red flag — Tractor Supply is transitioning from a high-growth retailer to a steady compounder. Whether this passes the growth filter depends on your threshold: the 5-year rates (7.9% revenue, 7.9% earnings) are close to double-digit but not there. The trend is not getting dramatically worse — it's stabilizing at a lower level.
