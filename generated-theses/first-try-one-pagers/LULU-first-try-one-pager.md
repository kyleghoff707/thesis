### Company Summary

Lululemon Athletica designs, distributes, and markets athletic apparel, footwear, and accessories for women, men, and girls. The company offers pants, shorts, tops, and jackets for both leisure and athletic activities such as yoga and running. Lululemon also sells fitness accessories, such as bags, yoga mats, and equipment. It sells its products through digital channels, a small number of wholesale partners, more than 760 company-owned stores in about two dozen countries in North America, Asia, and Western Europe, and about 40 franchised locations in the Middle East. The company was founded in 1998 and is based in Vancouver, Canada.

**How they make money:** Premium-priced athletic apparel sold through company-owned stores and e-commerce. No wholesale dependency. High gross margins (~57%) driven by brand power and vertical integration. Revenue is recurring — customers repurchase regularly.

**Why they might be durable:** Cult-like brand loyalty, premium positioning that resists discounting, vertical retail model (no middlemen), growing international presence, expanding men's category.

**Why they might not be:** Fashion risk (athleisure trend could fade), increasing competition (Nike, Alo Yoga, Vuori, On Running), premium pricing vulnerable in economic downturns, concentration in North America (~75% of revenue), recent growth deceleration.

**Clarity test:** Can you explain what this company does? Yes — they sell premium yoga pants and athletic apparel through their own stores and website. Simple and understandable. **PASS.**

#### Growth Assessment

**Positives:**
- Revenue has compounded at 20.3% over 5 years and 11.0% over 3 years — strong double-digit growth
- Equity/book value growth is consistent at 14-16% across all timeframes
- All 5-year and 3-year growth rates are solidly double-digit
- Gross margins are remarkably stable (55-59% band across 7 years)
- ROE consistently above 15%, averaging ~30% — exceptional capital allocation

**Concerns:**
- FY2025 shows deceleration: revenue growth slowed to 4.9%, earnings declined 13%, operating cash flow declined 29.5%
- FCF is volatile: $328M (2022) to $1,644M (2023) to $922M (2025)
- Operating margins compressed from 23.7% (2024) to 19.9% (2025)
- The 1-year growth rates are a clear step down from the 3-year and 5-year trends

**Verdict:** Growth has been outstanding over 3-5 years but is clearly decelerating in the most recent year. This is not yet a red flag — one year of slower growth after years of rapid expansion can be normal maturation. But it warrants close monitoring. If the deceleration continues into the next fiscal year, growth consistency fails.
