# Advanced Financial Statement Analysis

### Compressed Knowledge File for AI Reasoning

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Technical Summary

Objective: Determine whether a company is financially strong, growing
consistently, and capable of sustaining long-term compounding.

Core Evaluation Areas: 1. Income Statement (Growth + Profitability) 2.
Balance Sheet (Financial Health + Debt Discipline) 3. Cash Flow
Statement (Cash Reality Check) 4. AI as Analytical Assistant
(Validation + Efficiency)

Primary Growth Benchmark: \~15--20% consistent growth (Rule of 72 used
for doubling speed).

Debt Benchmark: Long-Term Debt \< 3× Net Income\
Long-Term Debt \< 3× Free Cash Flow

Liquidity Benchmark: Current Assets : Current Liabilities ≈ 2:1
preferred

Equity Growth Benchmark: Equity growth \> 10% annually

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Why Financial Statements Matter

Financial statements are the language of business.

They allow us to: - Evaluate past performance - Assess sustainability -
Project future growth potential

Three Core Statements: - Income Statement → Profitability over time -
Balance Sheet → Snapshot of financial health - Cash Flow Statement →
Actual movement of cash

Always verify company reporting currency for international companies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Income Statement Analysis

## 2.1 Revenue

Primary question: Is revenue growing consistently over time?

Process: - Review all available years - Look for steady upward trend -
Calculate doubling time using Rule of 72

Example benchmark: Doubling in 4 years ≈ 18% growth (72 ÷ 4 = 18%)

Consistency matters more than spikes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.2 Net Income

Key checks: - Use Net Income (Common Shareholders) if different - Is Net
Income keeping pace with Revenue? - Is margin stable?

Doubling benchmark: \~4 years ≈ 18% growth

If Net Income tracks Revenue → margins stable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.3 Earnings Per Share (EPS)

Purpose: Detect dilution or buybacks.

Interpretation: - Net Income growing faster than EPS → dilution - EPS
growing faster than Net Income → buybacks

Benchmark example: EPS doubling in \~3--4 years → 18--21% growth

EPS growth above Net Income often signals share buybacks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.4 Shares Outstanding

Question: Is the company reducing or increasing share count?

Preferred: Declining share count (buybacks).

Buybacks enhance EPS growth without requiring higher net income growth.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2.5 Income Statement Synthesis

Healthy pattern: - Revenue ≈ 18% growth - Net Income ≈ 18% growth - EPS
≈ 20%+ growth (buyback effect)

Clear revenue and earnings visibility precedes deeper analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Balance Sheet Analysis

## 3.1 Current Assets vs Current Liabilities

Snapshot test of financial strength.

Key ratio: Current Assets : Current Liabilities

Benchmarks: - 1:1 → acceptable - 2:1 → conservative and strong

Indicates ability to meet short-term obligations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3.2 Long-Term Debt

Questions: - Is LT debt rising or falling? - Has debt peaked and begun
declining?

Focus on: True long-term debt (not capital leases).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3.3 Long-Term Debt vs Cash Flow

Test 1: LT Debt \< 3× Net Income

Test 2: LT Debt \< 3× Free Cash Flow

If both pass → strong solvency position.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3.4 LT Debt vs Cash & Equivalents

Can company pay off debt with cash on hand?

If yes: Multiple layers of protection exist.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 3.5 Total Equity

Formula: Total Assets − Total Liabilities = Total Equity

Equity represents owner residual value.

Preferred: - Positive equity - Growing \>10% annually - Doubling in \~7
years (72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10%)

Equity alone is not a buy trigger, but must be healthy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Cash Flow Analysis

## 4.1 Net Income vs Cash Flow

Net Income can be distorted via accrual accounting.

Always validate with: - Cash from Operating Activities - Free Cash Flow
(FCF)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.2 Cash from Operating Activities

Harder to manipulate than earnings.

Check: - Consistent growth - Doubling speed (\~4 years ≈ 18%)

This is often where slowdown appears first.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.3 Free Cash Flow (FCF)

Definition: Cash remaining after capital expenditures.

Importance: Company can allocate FCF freely (buybacks, dividends, debt
repayment).

Key solvency test: LT Debt \< 3× FCF

If FCF slows: Investigate capital expenditures.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.4 Capital Expenditures (CapEx)

If FCF declines: Determine whether CapEx increased.

In some cases (e.g., AI investment), high CapEx may signal strategic
expansion rather than weakness.

Context matters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Integrated Financial Health Checklist

Healthy company profile:

-   Revenue growing 15--20%
-   Net Income tracking revenue
-   EPS ≥ Net Income growth
-   Shares declining (buybacks)
-   Current ratio near 2:1
-   LT Debt \< 3× Net Income
-   LT Debt \< 3× FCF
-   Equity growing \>10%
-   Operating Cash Flow stable and compounding
-   FCF positive and sufficient

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. AI as Analytical Assistant

AI is: - A tool - An assistant - Not a replacement

Error risk is real.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.1 AI Best Practices

-   Use structured sources (Excel, PDF, official reports)
-   Upload source data directly when possible
-   Double-check outputs
-   Verify against original financials
-   Normalize fiscal vs calendar year data

Garbage In → Garbage Out

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.2 Prompting Strategy

Effective prompts include:

1.  Clear role definition
2.  Specific task request
3.  Context and data boundaries

Use prompt chaining: Break complex tasks into sequential prompts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6.3 AI for Deeper Analysis

AI can assist with:

-   Identifying anomalies
-   Running "What if" scenarios
-   Stress testing ideas (inversion / rebuttals)
-   Extracting references
-   Comparing datasets for discrepancies

Always validate conclusions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Structural Insight

Financial statement analysis is layered:

Income Statement → Growth Quality\
Balance Sheet → Financial Stability\
Cash Flow → Cash Reality

AI accelerates analysis but does not replace understanding.

Master the "how" so you can validate the output.

End goal: Identify financially strong companies capable of long-term
compounding.
