# Pitch Deck IV

## Technical Summary

Focus: - 10-Year Industry & Company Outlook - Valuation Systems (MOS,
PBT, 10 Cap) - Margin of Safety Principle - Final Investment Conclusion

Objective: Determine what the business is worth, what we should pay, and
whether it offers sufficient margin of safety.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. 10-Year Outlook Framework

Before valuation, answer:

-   Will this industry exist and grow in 10 years?
-   Will this company still dominate?
-   Is its moat durable?
-   Is long-term growth rational and achievable?

If long-term durability is uncertain → valuation is irrelevant.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Efficient Market vs. Emotional Market

Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH): - Prices always reflect full
information. - Movement is random (coin flip).

Rule One Position: Markets are emotional. Mispricing creates
opportunity.

We require: - Wonderful company - Accurate valuation - Event causing
price drop - 50% discount (Margin of Safety)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Three Valuation Systems

Rule One uses three independent valuation methods:

1.  MOS (Margin of Safety) -- Earnings-based DCF-style
2.  PBT (Payback Time) -- Free Cash Flow based
3.  10 Cap -- Owner Earnings yield model

Different businesses require different lenses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Margin of Safety (MOS)

Built on Earnings.

### Step 1

Start with EPS (Trailing Twelve Months).

### Step 2

Project EPS forward 10 years using conservative Future Growth Rate
(FGR).

### Step 3

Future Price = Future EPS × Future P/E

Future P/E Rule: Future P/E ≈ 2 × Growth Rate Must be supported by
historical multiples.

### Step 4

Discount Future Value back at 15% required return. This produces the
Sticker Price.

### Step 5

MOS Price = 50% of Sticker.

Validation Requirements: - EPS linearity - Rational FGR - Future P/E
historically achievable

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Payback Time (PBT)

Concept: If we bought the entire company, how many years until free cash
flow pays back our purchase price?

Investors typically accept: 12--16 years payback Rule One prefers: ≤ 8
years (implies discount)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Free Cash Flow (FCF)

FCF = Operating Cash Flow -- Capital Expenditures

FCF Ratio: FCF / Net Income

Weighted average (10 years), giving more weight to recent years.

Outliers may be omitted if justified.

PBT outputs: - PBT Price - Sticker Price - Years to Payback at current
price

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. 10 Cap Valuation

Real-estate inspired yield method.

Cap Rate = Year 1 Yield on Purchase Price

10 Cap = 10% Owner Earnings yield

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Owner Earnings (OE)

OE = Operating Cash Flow\
-- Maintenance CapEx\
+ Tax Provision

Multiply OE × 10 = Total 10 Cap Purchase Price

Divide by Shares Outstanding → 10 Cap Price per share

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Maintenance CapEx Guidance

If breakdown unavailable: - Use company guidance if disclosed - Contact
Investor Relations - Conservative default: \~70% of total CapEx

During abnormal "event" years: Use last normal year values.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Valuation Interpretation

MOS → Growth-oriented lens\
PBT → Cash flow recovery lens\
10 Cap → Conservative yield lens

If multiple systems converge → higher conviction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Conclusion Section Framework

Final Pitch Deck should answer:

-   Is this a wonderful business?
-   Is management capable?
-   Is growth rational?
-   Are risks rebutted?
-   Is valuation conservative?
-   Is price sufficiently discounted?

If yes across systems → candidate for capital allocation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
