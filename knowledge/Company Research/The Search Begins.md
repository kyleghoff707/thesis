# Fundamentals -- The Search Begins

## Technical Summary

> **Primary Topics:** Long-term investing mindset, self-knowledge assessment, finding companies to invest in, Rule One ToolBox scanning, guru portfolios, Rule One Investing One Pager, minimum standards for investment, Meaning and Management KPIs
>
> **Key concept:** Rulers use two mindsets -- short-term trading requires "probability" in your favor, while long-term investing requires "certainty" through understanding
>
> **Key concept:** The Three Circles exercise (Passions, Talent, Money) identifies industries and sectors where you have personal competence to evaluate companies
>
> **Key concept:** Self-assessment and personal values are the starting filter for finding investable companies -- invest in what you know and understand
>
> **Key concept:** Three methods to search for stocks -- Scan for Stocks (ToolBox sector/industry filters, debt ratios, Rule One scores), News (macro events, troubled stocks as opportunities), and Gurus (SEC 13-F filings from big money managers)
>
> **Key concept:** The Rule One Investing One Pager is a quick filtering tool with Company Information, Minimum Standards (market cap >$200M, US HQ, public >7 years, debt <3 years earnings), Meaning KPIs (3 Circles, Gurus, Rule One Score, Bigger in 10 Years), and Management KPIs (ROE >=10%, ROIC >=10%, Net Debt to Earnings <3 years, Net Debt to FCF <3 years)
>
> **Key concept:** Growth Metrics -- look for consistent growth across Book Value, Earnings, Operating Cash, Revenue, and Free Cash Flow over a long period of time
>
> **Key concept:** If a company fails Minimum Standards, Meaning KPIs, Management KPIs, Company Summary understanding, or Growth Rates -- move on
>
> **Trades Referenced:** N/A (this is a fundamentals/stock research webinar, not a trading strategies webinar)
>
> **Example Company:** Costco (COST) -- used throughout as the One Pager walkthrough example

## Rulers Use Two Mindsets

- Rulers can be both short term traders and long term investors.
- Short term trading requires putting "probability" in our favor.
- Long term investing requires "certainty."

*[Illustration: Two silhouette heads -- one labeled "ST Trading Vigilance" and the other "LT Investing Patience"]*

---

## Long Term Investing Mindset

- Rule One is about gaining investment **"CERTAINTY."**
- "CERTAINTY" comes from **UNDERSTANDING.**
- We must **UNDERSTAND** the company and industry we are investing in to buy it on sale.

---

## Successful Investing Requires Self-Knowledge

> *"Know thyself."* -- Socrates

- How do we know that we can understand a company?
- We need to know what we know and know what we don't know.

---

## Self Assessment Exercise

- What interests and passions do you have?
- What training and competency do you have?
- How do you make your money and what do you spend it on?

## Rule One Investing: Your Values Matter

- Your personal values are a great filter in the search for companies.
- Find companies that reflect your values, or at least do not break them.
- Do you love this company and do you want to see more of it in the world?

## Investing Your Values

- Does the company's mission and purpose match your values?

## Company Summary

What does this company do?

> **Company Summary:**
>
> As the leading warehouse club, Costco has 838 stores worldwide (at the end of fiscal 2022), with most sales derived in the United States (73%) and Canada (14%). It sells memberships that allow customers to shop in its warehouses, which feature low prices on a limited product assortment. Costco mainly caters to individual shoppers, but nearly 20% of paid members carry business memberships. Food and sundries accounted for nearly 39% of fiscal 2022 sales, with non-food merchandise 27%, warehouse ancillary and other businesses (such as fuel and pharmacy) 21%, and fresh food 13%. Costco's warehouses average around 146,000 square feet; over 75% of its locations offer fuel. About 7% of Costco's global sales come from e-commerce (excluding same-day grocery and various other services).
