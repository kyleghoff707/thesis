# Financial Statements and Future Growth Rate

## Technical Summary

Objective: Determine a rational, conservative, and achievable Future
Growth Rate (FGR) using financial statement analysis and forward-looking
research.

Core Components: 1. Income Statement 2. Balance Sheet 3. Cash Flow
Statement 4. Big 4 Growth Rates 5. Market Relativity 6. Company & Sector
Guidance 7. Analysts' Consensus 8. Rule of 72 Rationality Check

FGR is not a formula --- it is an informed assessment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Financial Statement Foundations

Financial statements are standardized (GAAP) and allow:

-   Cross-company comparison
-   Performance tracking
-   Financial strength evaluation

Three Core Statements:

Income Statement → Profitability over time\
Balance Sheet → Financial position snapshot\
Cash Flow Statement → Actual cash movement

Cash is more important than accounting earnings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Income Statement Structure

Revenue -- Cost of Revenue = Gross Profit\
Gross Profit -- Operating Expenses = Operating Income\
Operating Income -- Interest -- Taxes = Net Income

Key Questions:

-   Is revenue growing consistently?
-   Is net income tracking revenue?
-   Are margins stable?
-   Is EPS growing faster than net income (buybacks)?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Balance Sheet Structure

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Assets: - Current (convertible within 1 year) - Non-current (long-term)

Liabilities: - Current (due within 1 year) - Long-term

Equity: Residual ownership value.

Focus Areas: - Current Ratio (Current Assets / Current Liabilities) -
Equity trend over time - Debt sustainability

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Cash Flow Statement Structure

Three Sections:

Operating Cash Flow\
Investing Cash Flow\
Financing Cash Flow

Operating Cash Flow: Net Income\
+ Non-cash items (Depreciation)\
+ Changes in Working Capital

Free Cash Flow (FCF): Operating Cash -- CapEx

FCF is the foundation of valuation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. P/E and Growth Relationship

Rule of Thumb: Reasonable P/E ≈ 2 × Growth Rate

Historical Average P/E ≈ 16\
→ Implies \~8% average growth

Examples: 8% growth → \~16 P/E reasonable\
15% growth → \~30 P/E reasonable

Always compare P/E to sector averages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. The Big 4 Growth Rates

Used together to prevent distortion:

-   Equity Growth
-   Net Income Growth
-   EPS Growth
-   Operating Cash Flow Growth

Toolbox calculates: - 10-year weighted averages - Composite Growth
Rate - Analyst long-term estimate

Outliers must be identified and removed if justified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Creating a Future Growth Rate (FGR)

FGR must be achievable every year for 10 years.

Perspectives:

1.  Rear View Mirror (historical Big 4 trends)
2.  ROE & ROIC sustainability
3.  Market Relativity (S&P \~7.5% real CAGR)
4.  Company Guidance (SEC filings)
5.  Sector Growth Outlook
6.  Analysts' Consensus

Example Framework: Past Growth: 17.85%\
Market: 7.5%\
Company Guidance: 4--5%\
Sector: 2--3%\
Analysts: 8.9%

Final FGR must be conservative and rational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Spot Check (Rule of 72)

Use Rule of 72 to:

-   Estimate doubling speed
-   Project revenue in 10 years
-   Compare future market share

If projected market share expansion is unrealistic → lower FGR.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Evidence + Conclusions = FGR

Principles:

-   Be conservative
-   Avoid optimism bias
-   Recalculate when new data emerges
-   FGR is an average across good and bad years

"Optimism is the enemy of good investing."
