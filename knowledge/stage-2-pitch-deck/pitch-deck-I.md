# The Pitch Deck

## Technical Summary

Purpose: The Pitch Deck is a structured research framework used to
evaluate a company before capital deployment.

It breaks company analysis into 10 areas:

1.  Radar
2.  Simple & Predictable
3.  Dominant Market Position
4.  Large Barrier to Entry
5.  Free Cash Flow Generative
6.  Management Integrity & Talent
7.  High Returns (ROE / ROIC / ROA)
8.  Strong Balance Sheet
9.  Limited Exposure to P.E.S.T Risk
10. Valuation

(Source: The Pitch Deck.PDF)

Objective: Move from surface-level screening to deep conviction through
organized research.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Radar

Initial Awareness & Context

Key Questions: - How did we discover the company? (Three Circles, News,
Search) - Have respected investors ("Gurus") owned it? - Is there a
current event causing mispricing? - What specifically happened?

Event Analysis: - Identify price drop magnitude. - Research cause
(earnings revision, guidance cut, macro issue). - Compare to past
dislocations. - Determine if event is temporary or structural.

Radar is about context, not conviction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Simple & Predictable

Simple: Can you clearly explain how the company makes money?

Predictable: Does history show steady growth and consistent performance?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Business Model Clarity

Must be able to describe: - Revenue sources - Customer base - Value
proposition - Core product/service focus

If explanation is unclear → "Too Hard" box.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Predictability Tests

-   10-year revenue trend
-   Earnings consistency
-   Cash flow stability
-   Margin durability

"The best predictor of the future is the past."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Cyclicality

Cyclical industries are allowed. Must understand: - Where company sits
in cycle - Revenue volatility patterns - Recession performance

Cyclicality ≠ disqualification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Problem Solved

Identify: - What problem does the company solve? - Is the problem
durable? - Can claims be verified? - Has company succeeded solving it?

Strong companies solve enduring problems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Dominant Market Position

Dominance: Power and influence within an industry.

We prefer companies with: - Significant competitive advantages - Clear
niche positioning - Historical profitability strength

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Moat Score Concept

History of profitability signals dominance.

Compare: - Revenue growth vs competitors - Return metrics vs
competitors - Market share vs competitors

Strong moat = sustained superiority.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Niche Identification

Determine: - What makes the company different? - What segment does it
dominate? - Is it Top 3 in its niche?

Niche clarity strengthens competitive advantage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Market Size & Share

Research: - Total addressable market (TAM) - Company revenue vs industry
size - Market share trend (growing or shrinking)

Growth of market share matters more than static dominance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Competitor Comparison

Identify: - Direct competitors - Indirect competitors - Private
competitors (may require deeper filings research)

Compare: - Revenue growth - Profitability - Return metrics - Market
positioning

Superior financial metrics often reflect real dominance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Research Discipline

Strong research habits include:

-   Reading industry materials
-   Reviewing competitor filings
-   Searching for hidden data
-   Saving investor presentations
-   Validating management claims

Deep research builds conviction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Six-Inch Bar Concept

Question: Is this company easy enough to understand that you can step
over it like a six-inch bar?

If not: - Too complex - Too unpredictable - Outside circle of competence

Simplicity is a strength.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Structural Insight

The Pitch Deck organizes research so no critical question is skipped.

Sequence:

Radar → Context\
Simple & Predictable → Business Clarity\
Dominance → Competitive Strength

Only after these pass should deeper analysis (growth rates, valuation,
strategy) proceed.

The Pitch Deck is not about speed. It is about structured conviction.
