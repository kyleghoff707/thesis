The authors found that the robotic-assisted group had a 55% lower risk of local recurrence at three years compared to the laparoscopic group. The authors concluded that robotic surgery significantly improved long-term oncological outcomes for patients with middle or low rectal cancer compared to conventional laparoscopic surgery. With more real-world clinical data and enhanced training programs for surgeons, robotic surgery could become the preferred choice for these patients. With the launch of the da Vinci 5 platform, we are starting to see evidence that new features such as force feedback technology and case insights may add value to minimally invasive surgery. At this month’s Society of Robotic Surgery Annual Meeting, Dr. Laila Rashidi from MultiCare Health System shared her findings. She examined the relationship between clinical outcomes and objective performance indicators during her single surgeon experience, particularly focusing on the force measured during surgery using force feedback instruments. To conduct this analysis, Dr. Rashidi and her colleagues utilized OPIs generated by Case Insights, an AI-enabled solution that combines surgical videos with data from the da Vinci platform. Their evaluation of 28 low anterior resection cases showed that certain patient factors and OPIs were linked to outcomes in these colorectal procedures. Notably, higher forces, especially during dissection, and previous surgeries correlated with longer hospital stays. The strongest predictor of length of stay was the percentage of force time above 6.5 newtons. Dr. Rashidi and her team concluded that their early experience with the dV5 highlights the association between patient factors, OPIs, and outcomes in robotic-assisted low anterior resection.
