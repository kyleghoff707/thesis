**Michael J. Arougheti** *(CEO)*:

It's a great question. There's a long-standing narrative in the market, which has persisted for 30 years, suggesting that private credit is risky while public credit is not, leading to expectations of continuous credit loss in private markets. However, we have not experienced that. Our public track record during the global financial crisis, COVID, and periods of rate volatility supports this point. The annualized loss rates in our direct lending portfolios hover around 10 basis points and have remained stable for an extended period. The notion that direct lending has not been tested through cycles is something I strongly disagree with. The past few years of increased base rate environments have actually benefited the asset class. Our senior direct lending has achieved a 14% return, European direct lending 11%, and opportunistic credit funds close to 16%. Unsurprisingly, this performance is drawing capital away from traditional fixed income, and I believe these returns are sustainable.  The current performance uptick is due to the improving quality of companies entering private markets as awareness about them as a financing solution grows. Additionally, we're coming off a vintage where equity contributions to leveraged capital structures are at record levels, making loan-to-value ratios a strong buffer against potential losses. Even if we see earnings decline, the significant equity subordination in these markets should help to mitigate losses. I don't foresee an uptick in credit loss.  Moreover, borrowers are increasingly recognizing the benefits of private markets; they pursue bilateral relationships with lenders, allowing them to react swiftly when executing their business plans and resolve issues collaboratively during challenging times. In public markets, the dynamic is different, where bondholders might try to take control of a company when they buy loans at a discount. There's a notable shift in the market where borrowers prefer to stay in the private space, contributing to positive performance since there are more options to address potential losses in a bilateral relationship. The duration of private credit capital, alongside the fact that most private credit funds are either unlevered or have low leverage, tends to reduce market volatility. Despite experiencing rate fluctuations, we haven't seen significant drawdowns in liquid markets, likely due to the strength of private markets. Government interventions have also played a role in stabilizing the markets, but private credit has been instrumental. While I wouldn't say I look forward to a typical credit cycle, should one occur, it may serve as a chance to further demonstrate the durability of this asset class. I believe we'll outperform again during challenging market conditions.
